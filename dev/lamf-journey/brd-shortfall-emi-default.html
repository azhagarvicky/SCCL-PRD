BRD – Shortfall & EMI Default

DraftLast updated 17-11-2026 · EMI bounce, re-presentation, charges and the withdrawal freeze; LTV breach alerts at 90% and invocation at 75%; invocation reasons in LMS, maker-checker with OTP, and loan closure with refund of the excess.

1. Overview

This BRD covers what happens when a customer misses an EMI (EMI default) and when the value of the pledged funds falls below what the loan needs (shortfall, also called LTV breach). It covers the charges, the re-presentation of the EMI, the customer notifications, the withdrawal freeze, the invocation (sale) of the pledged units and the loan closure.

2. EMI default

  1. The EMI is presented on the 5th of every month. If it bounces, it is presented again on the 10th of the same month.
  2. A bounce charge of ₹100 + GST is levied for every bounce. Penal charges of 36% a year are levied on the unpaid interest / EMI from the first day of default until the dues are paid.
  3. The next EMI is presented as usual on its due date. The penal and bounce charges of the earlier default are posted to the customer’s ledger and, if the customer does not pay them, are collected at the time of loan closure.
  4. If the next EMI also bounces on its due date, it is presented again 5 days later (T+5).
  5. If the T+5 re-presentation also bounces, the customer has missed the EMI for two months in a row. The funds are then sold (invoked), the outstanding is settled, the loan is closed, and any amount left over after the sale is refunded to the customer.
  6. For a loan closed through invocation, the pending interest, the charges, the outstanding principal and the lien removal charges are recovered.
  7. The invocation is done manually by the team, not triggered automatically by the system.
  8. The customer gets an SMS and an email at every stage above.

3. Shortfall (LTV breach)

LTV % = (Qualified loan amount ÷ Total loan outstanding) × 100

Qualified loan amount = sum of (latest collateral value of each fund × LTV of that fund type), where the LTV depends on whether the fund is debt or equity.

LTV %What happens
Reaches 90%The customer gets an SMS and an email every day asking them to pay the shortfall amount or to pledge more funds to cover it.
Between 90% and 75%No invocation. The loan stays as it is even if the customer does not pay, unless there is an internal decision to sell the units.
Reaches 75%The funds are sold to recover the shortfall amount and the lien removal charges for every invocation. The loan stays ACTIVE, because only the shortfall and its charges are recovered.
  • To cover the shortfall by payment, the customer pays the exact shortfall amount.
  • To cover the shortfall by pledging, the customer pledges funds worth 2 times the shortfall amount.
  • The invocation is done manually by the team, not triggered automatically by the system.

4. Withdrawal rules

4.1 During an LTV breach

  • The Withdraw option is disabled in LOS, as the drawing power is less than the outstanding amount.

4.2 During an EMI default

  • If the EMI bounces at the first presentation of any month, the account is frozen for withdrawal.
  • If the payment is received at the re-presentation, the freeze is released and the customer can withdraw again.
  • If the re-presentation also bounces, the customer cannot withdraw until they pay the dues.
  • Until the overdue interest / EMI is paid, the withdrawal stays frozen, even when the defaults are not in consecutive months.

7. Loan closure through invocation

  1. The units are sold (redeemed) to recover the total outstanding amount.
  2. The amount left over after recovery is refunded to the customer. It is recorded under Refund even when it is zero.
  3. New fields under the Refund category, against the redemption details: UTR number, refund date and amount refunded.
  4. The refund (points 2 and 3) is also posted in the customer ledger.
  5. Once the total outstanding is recovered and the excess is refunded, the loan account is updated to CLOSED.
  6. A loan can come into the revocation (de-pledge) window only when the total loan outstanding is 0, or is in credit because of an excess payment.
